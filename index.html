Philosophy

Good foundations save time. Great foundations save headaches.

Developers shouldn’t have to begin every project from absolute zero. A template should be more than a quick demo: it should be a considered starting point that you can read, understand, and adapt.

Browse a structure, pick the one that fits, make small adjustments, and take the source code with you. It’s yours to own, and it rests on a clean, maintained foundation instead of a pile of one-off decisions.

  • Structured

    Clear layout and sensible conventions from the first commit.

  • Maintainable

    Built to be understood by the next person, including future you.

  • Security-conscious

    Careful defaults that reduce risk from the start.

  • Ready to adapt

    Small, honest changes get you to something that’s yours.

How it works

    Choose a foundation.

    Browse the collection and pick the structure closest to what you’re building.

  2. 02

    Make it yours.

    Adjust the content, styling, and configuration. Small changes, not a rewrite.

  3. 03

    Build something.

    Take the source, own it, and build on a foundation that’s already organized.

Maintenance

Made to last, maintained in the open.

Security-conscious defaults
Templates start from careful, conservative choices and aim to reduce common risks.
Clean, understandable code
Readable over clever. If you can’t follow it, you can’t own it.
Community maintenance
Contributors keep templates current, review changes, and fix what breaks.
Transparent development
Decisions, issues, and changes happen publicly, where anyone can follow along.
Long-term maintainability
Fewer dependencies and simpler structure make upkeep realistic.

No template is guaranteed to be free of vulnerabilities. Good defaults lower risk; they don’t replace review, testing, and keeping your own project up to date.
